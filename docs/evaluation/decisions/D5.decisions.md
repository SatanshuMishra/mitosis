# Decisions for RFC 3339

Questions the document left open, settled. These are binding.

- The day-of-week computation refuses a date before 0000-03-01 rather than
  computing it, because the Appendix B sample agrees with Python integer
  division only for nonnegative operands.

- The document's leap-second table ships as an immutable tuple of rows
  holding the UTC date and the TAI-UTC value after it. Every function takes
  an optional table argument so later announcements are supplied by the
  caller without mutating the module.

- A row whose TAI-UTC value is lower than the previous row's marks a
  subtracted leap second with maximum second 58. The first row of any table
  is an insertion.

- TAI-UTC is answered only for a date that is a row. Any other date is
  refused rather than interpolated.

- A clock shift whose result leaves the 0000 through 9999 era is refused.

- Lower-case `t` and `z` are accepted by default, and a flag demands upper
  case for case-sensitive environments. A separator other than `T` is
  refused unless the caller passes the separators to allow.

- Fixed ranges stated in the ABNF comments are checked in the grammar.
  Limits that depend on context, the day by month and year and second 60 by
  the leap-second table, are checked in the timestamp layer. Both layers
  keep their own check; neither relies on the other.

- The parts record is a frozen named tuple: year, month, day, hour, minute
  and second as ints, fraction as the digit string after the dot or None,
  offset as None for Z or a sign-hours-minutes tuple otherwise.

- Offset holds signed minutes computed as local minus UTC, plus a flag set
  only by `-00:00`. Z and `+00:00` are one value and both format as Z, while
  `-00:00` formats as `-00:00`.

- Offset hours are limited to 00-23 and minutes to 00-59.

- Timestamp equality is structural over every field, so Z and `-00:00`
  spellings differ. Instant comparison is the separate named operation
  `same_instant`, and the ordering operators compare the wall clock shifted
  to UTC together with the fraction as a number. These are two distinct
  operations and neither is renamed to the other.

- The fraction is kept as the digit string given, so rendering round-trips
  the input precision. Rendering always writes a four-digit zero-padded year
  and upper-case T and Z.

- A second of 60 is accepted only when the wall clock shifted to UTC reads
  23:59 on a row of the leap-second table. A maximum of 58 applies only on a
  subtracted-leap-second row.

- Converting a timestamp carrying `-00:00` to UTC relabels it Z without
  shifting. A second of 60 survives either conversion.

- Canonical text converts to UTC, spells the zone as Z, and pads the fraction
  with zeros to exactly the requested digit count. Trailing zeros beyond that
  count are dropped, a nonzero digit beyond it is refused rather than
  rounded, and zero requested digits omits the fraction entirely.

- Year repair models the broken software of section 3 as year minus 1900,
  written either as decimal digits or as one ASCII character followed by one
  digit. Four digits return unchanged, three digits gain 1900, and a
  two-character form decodes as 1900 plus ten times its decade plus the final
  digit. Anything else is refused.

- The strict parser never calls year repair. It is an explicit opt-in for
  logging-style callers.
